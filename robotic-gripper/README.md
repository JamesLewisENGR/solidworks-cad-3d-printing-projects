# Robotic Gripper — SolidWorks Design & 3D-Printed Prototype

![Robotic Gripper SolidWorks Design](first%20photo%20of%20robot%20gripper.png)

**Status: In Progress**

## Project Overview

I am developing a robotic gripper in SolidWorks that I plan to manufacture using 3D printing and continue modifying as I test the physical design.

I used several YouTube videos and existing gripper designs as references for the initial concept and mechanism. From those references, I created my own SolidWorks model and overall assembly. My goal with this project is not to stop at the initial CAD model, but to use it as the starting point for a physical prototype that I can test, modify, and continue developing.

The overall CAD design is complete, and I am currently moving into the 3D-printing and physical testing stage.

🎥 **[Watch the Robotic Gripper Build Overview on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=37y65f_CWxo)**

## CAD Design

I modeled the gripper components in SolidWorks and assembled them to create the overall mechanism. While working on the CAD design, I had to think about how the individual parts connect, how the mechanism moves, and how the dimensions of the components will affect the physical assembly.

This project has given me additional experience with multi-part mechanical design rather than only modeling individual parts.

## Reference Designs

I used a few YouTube videos as a basis and reference while developing the initial gripper concept. I am documenting that because the original mechanism was influenced by existing designs rather than being developed completely from scratch.

The project is continuing beyond those references. I am creating and testing my own physical version, and I plan to revise dimensions and add to the design based on what I learn from the printed prototype.

## 3D Printing & Physical Testing

The next stage is to 3D print the components and assemble the physical gripper. Once I have the printed parts in front of me, I will be able to evaluate things that are harder to judge from CAD alone, including fit, clearances, movement, alignment, and how well the components work together.

If a dimension or fit does not work the way I expected, I plan to go back into SolidWorks, change the design, reprint the part, and test it again.

My development process for the physical prototype will be:

**CAD design → 3D print → assemble → test → identify problems → revise dimensions/design → reprint → retest**

## Planned Development

As I continue the project, I plan to:

- 3D print the current gripper design
- Assemble and test the physical mechanism
- Evaluate fit and movement between components
- Adjust dimensions and clearances based on the printed parts
- Make CAD revisions where needed
- Add additional features/components to the original design
- Document major design changes and why I made them
- Record the finished prototype and its movement
